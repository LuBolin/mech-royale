Worth noting:
• Upgrade nanites (+60% regen, starts 1.5 s sooner) directly powers F2. Strong pick for a survival bot.
• Laser + lightning is forgiving for a bot with rough aim: one is hitscan, the other auto-targets in a cone.
• Sounds name the weapon. A lightning sound means keep that mech beyond 45 m; a laser_charge sound means break LOS.
• Cubes trade size for power. Late game, a 10-cube mech is 50% bigger and easier to hit.
Navigation with elevation
Use a multi-layer nav graph, not a flat grid, because mechs can jump, jet onto roofs and walk under bridges. Build it once at docker build time from arena/v2/world.json and terrain.i16.
Inputs:
• terrain.i16: little-endian int16 centimetres, 1070 × 724, 1 m step from (terrain.minX, terrain.minY). Sample bilinearly.
• prisms[]: buildings, stadium, bridges. Each has base, top, a footprint ring and holes. Bridges have base above ground.
• Ignore blocks, cars, parking, water, barriers: scenery only, they block nothing.
Graph:
• Nodes: surface cells at ~2 m on every walkable surface: terrain, each roof (top), each bridge deck. The ground under a bridge is a separate layer from its deck when the gap clears 9 m.
• Walk edges: neighbours with height difference ≤ 1.2 m, slope ≤ 50°, and 9 m head clearance.
• Drop edges: step off any edge to a lower surface. Cheap: there is no fall damage in the rules.
• Climb edges: from a ground cell beside a wall to the roof edge. Cost = time + energy (≈ 1.5 × height + margin). Usable only when current energy covers it; keep a reserve for escapes.
• Out of reach: roofs above ~60 m (the ~70 m towers) unless capacitor/jets upgrades raise the budget.
• Mech footprint: inflate walls by the 2.5 m radius (scaled by cubes later) so paths don't scrape corners.
Precomputed per cell (for strategy): height, roof flag, cover score (how much nearby geometry blocks LOS), exposure (how many directions can see it). These drive hiding spots, regen spots and overwatch spots.
At runtime:
• Zone flow field: once per zone phase, compute distance-to-safe-circle over the whole graph (spread across ticks). Each tick, step to the neighbour with the lowest value.
• Local A*: short searches for loot, cover, drops; budgeted per tick.
• Physics gotchas: walls slide you along them; you can land on roof edges; eaves stop jets. Expect path-following to need correction from observed pos/vel.
Architecture and the 50 ms budget
TypeScript on Bun, hand-written logic in four layers, with all map work done at docker build time. The per-tick cost is parsing plus the reflex layer; everything heavy runs on a slower clock or is precomputed.
The arrows show data flow inside one decision. Strategy and pathing don't run every tick, so most ticks go straight from world model to reflexes.
Language: any language works (it's just a Docker image on stdin/stdout). Bun gives JIT speed with typed arrays and the kit's TS wrapper is ready to use. Rust/Go are faster with steadier timing, but need a ~50-line JSON-lines loop written from mechfight.schema.json. Python is too slow for pathfinding on a ~775k-cell map. Parsing is not the bottleneck: observations average ~1.4 KB, max ~7 KB.
Performance rules:
• Target ≤ 20 ms per tick, leaving room for pipe latency and the shared CPU.
• Check the clock inside heavy loops; when out of time, send the reflex action and resume next tick. Never send nothing.
• Use a 2 m grid (~190k cells) and typed arrays; reuse buffers, avoid allocating objects in hot loops (GC pauses).
• LOS: test only the few tracked objects per tick; a coarse "which prisms are in this cell" table so each ray tests nearby buildings, not all 259.
• Build-time output lives in the image, so it doesn't count toward the repo's 20 MB limit.
